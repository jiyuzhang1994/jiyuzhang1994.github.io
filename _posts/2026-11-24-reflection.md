I am writing some thoughts as a reflection for myself about AI mathematics and beyond.  

Human and AI are different in their computational limitations. AI has more memory and and is better at mechanically tracking calculations while humans do have limitations on that. Consider the task of verifying a mathematical proof. First we should make it clear that proof = computation. A proof could be in the form of a long sequence of  calculations and it can be hard to track the steps and do the verification.  We human tend to simplify the complication raised from the computation, by identifying symmetric mathematical structures, by abstracting and isolating  lemmas, so that the proof can be made simpler, cleaner and and usually more intuitive, and in this process we claim to have better understanding of the structure. Similarly, when we are solving a complex problem for which the computation is far less tractable in current techniques, we would often need to set intermediate goals, invent new notions/theories [^1], and as we gain more insight and get better understanding along the way, we would produce cleaner, shorter proofs. AI does not seem to have this limitations. For example, if a calculation needs to be expanded to a huge number of terms even though many of them cancelled each other eventually, AI would just do the calculation, and human would try to find symmetric structure within the terms and simplify the calculation.

[^1]: one way is to start with *reductions*, so that we can transform the problems into familiar instances in the hope that known techniques are useful. However, as is known to all, reductions do not always make life easier. )

Even within human, people differ in their calculation abilities. Some people are better at mental math, or are better at tracking complicated calculations. Other people need to put a lot of effort in simplifying the calculations, understanding what it is doing before move to the next stage.
